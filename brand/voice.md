# Forspring voice and messaging

How Forspring sounds and what it says: sites, product pages, emails, decks, product chrome and the texts
agents write for us. Section 7 of the [brand guideline](README.md) is the short version; this is the full one.

Three sources sit above this file, in the hub (forspringai/forspring):

- **The [founders' letter](https://github.com/forspringai/forspring/blob/main/docs/founders-letter.md).**
  Where this file and the letter disagree, the letter wins. The letter changes only with Peter.
- **[docs/brand/vision.md](https://github.com/forspringai/forspring/blob/main/docs/brand/vision.md)**
  (hub D-115): what Forspring and each product is, in approved texts, and the words table. Copy those texts
  word for word; this file does not repeat them.
- **[docs/offer.md](https://github.com/forspringai/forspring/blob/main/docs/offer.md):** prices and packages.

This replaces the retired site's copy rules (section 4, pair 1), which sold a kind of worker. That framing is
gone (hub D-115). Forspring delivers results, not licences.

## 1. Who we sound like

Four traits, each taken from the letter.

**People who built businesses and like helping others grow theirs.** Which means: we write to someone
running a company, as people who have run one, and we talk about their business before ours.

> "We loved building our own businesses, and since then we have loved helping others grow theirs."

**Direct.** Which means: the point comes first, in a short sentence, and then we stop. Two words can be a
sentence.

> "That time is over." ... "Not anymore."

**Honest about the problem.** Which means: we describe the reader's situation the way they would, concretely,
with no scare words and no flattery.

> "Most companies run on a patchwork of software that doesn't fit together, and on manual processes that are
> slow, error-prone and expensive in staff time."

**Accountable for the result.** Which means: we say what we will deliver and own it. We never hand over a tool
and leave the customer to make it work.

> "We believe we should deliver results, and get paid for them."

The voice stays the same everywhere; the tone moves with the place. A site page can take its time and be warm.
Product chrome is brief (section 6).

## 2. The message

One structure for everything we say about Forspring. Pages, decks and emails pick from it; none adds to it.

**Why now (the ground).** AI can make almost any business better, but most companies can't reach it yet. What
used to take a big IT department no longer does.

> "Both used to need a big IT department and a big software budget. Not anymore."

**The one claim: results, not licences.** We deliver a result and get paid for it. We don't sell a licence or a
tool the customer is left to make work.

> "But not as a licence you pay for every month and are left to make work. That time is over. A solution you
> still have to make work yourself is not enough either."

**The two starting points.**

1. **A true picture of the business**, so the customer can make the right decisions: one set of numbers
   everyone agrees on.
2. **Automating the manual work** that eats the team's time.

> "We usually start in two places: a true picture of your business, so you can make the right decisions, and
> automating the manual work that eats your team's time."

**The proof style: how we back the claim.**

- **Our people, who understand your business.** People who learn how the customer works and build for them.
  We build; we don't advise (no "consultants", "strategy").
- **Our own building blocks.** Spring, BrainSpring, DataSpring, SiteSpring, AppSpring and PulseSpring, which
  we run on ourselves first (vision.md: forspring.ai runs on SiteSpring, Forspring's own org is AppSpring's
  first in production). Name a block when it helps the reader; never make the blocks the headline of
  Forspring copy.
- **You buy the castle.** Show the finished thing for this customer: the numbers they can trust, the work that
  no longer lands on a person. Not the parts list.

> "But you don't buy the Lego blocks. You buy the castle we build for you."

**The one line**, from vision.md, word for word:

> Forspring gives companies what a world-class IT team would build for them: a true picture of the business
> and less manual work, assembled from our own agentic building blocks and paid for by results.

The other texts, Danish included, are in vision.md. A product's own site leads with its own one line, says it
is part of Forspring (hub D-115 item 4), and may say more for partners and technical teams.

## 3. Rules for copy

- **Plain words, short sentences.** One idea per sentence. A technical word is explained in the body or left
  out of the headline.
- **"We" to "you".** Forspring is "we"; the customer is "you" ("I/jer" in Danish, section 5).
- **Who it is for:** "small and mid-sized companies", not necessarily Danish ones (hub D-115). Forspring itself
  is a Danish company.
- **Use the words table.** What to say and what to avoid is in vision.md's
  [Words](https://github.com/forspringai/forspring/blob/main/docs/brand/vision.md#words) table; follow it, don't
  copy it here.
- **No hype.** Beyond the words table, avoid: revolutionary, seamless, unlock, supercharge, game-changer,
  cutting-edge, next-generation, state-of-the-art, transform, empower, effortless, magic, 10x. In Danish:
  revolutionerende, banebrydende, gnidningsfri, næste generation. "World-class" appears only as the letter
  uses it, about the IT team a company could have.
- **Numbers with sources.** Every figure names its publisher and year and links to a live source. A
  comparison is checked again before the page is edited.
- **No invented proof.** No made-up clients, numbers, logos, quotes, case studies or scarcity. A reference
  customer is named only when a decision or vision.md names them.
- **No AI-employee or chatbot framing.** No AI employees, AI workforce, hiring levels or "hire an agent".
  Spring is the "AI colleague" and is the only product called that. The letter calls chatbots the simple
  end of AI; we are not one.
- **No saved-hours headline.** Lead with the result (a true picture, less manual work, the bottom line), not
  a count of hours. The letter speaks of work that "eats your team's time", not hours on a meter.
- **AI is the means, never the product.** AI explains why this is possible now and how the blocks work. The
  headline is what the customer gets. "Agentic" describes the building blocks, as vision.md does; it is not a
  selling point on its own, and "AI-powered" is never the headline.
- **Price.** Prices live in [offer.md](https://github.com/forspringai/forspring/blob/main/docs/offer.md); copy
  them, never invent one. Never frame the price as a licence, a seat or a per-user subscription. "Get paid for
  results" is our stance; what it means for pricing is still open (vision.md, "Paid for results"), so copy
  does not promise a pricing model offer.md has not settled.
- **One call to action per page,** said the same way everywhere it appears (README section 10).

## 5. Danish

- **Customer-facing copy is Danish** (hub D-7); customer screens are Danish first with English available, one
  language per page (hub `docs/ui-standard.md`). Internal docs and operator screens may be English.
- **Product names stay as written** in Danish copy: Spring, BrainSpring, DataSpring, SiteSpring, AppSpring,
  PulseSpring. They join a Danish compound with a hyphen, as the chrome writes "Din Forspring-konto".
- **Addressing (decided by Peter, 2026-11-11, hub D-118).** Marketing and sales copy
  addresses the company as **"I/jer"** (vision.md's "jeres Slack"); product UI addresses the person in front of
  it as **"du"**, as `@forspring/ui` already does ("Du er ikke logget ind") and with "hos jer" where it means
  the company ("Bed den, der står for ... hos jer, om at invitere dig."). Never "De".
- **"Small and mid-sized companies"** is "små og mellemstore virksomheder".
- **The Danish lines in vision.md are drafts for the copywriters,** not finished copy. Match their choices
  until a copywriter changes them there:
  - "Spring er AI-kollegaen i jeres Slack, der udfører arbejdet ud fra det, virksomheden ved." (AI-kollega,
    not AI-medarbejder; "jeres" for the company)
  - "et sandt billede af forretningen og mindre manuelt arbejde" (a true picture; less manual work)
  - "sat sammen af vores egne agentiske byggeklodser og betalt for resultater" (byggeklodser for building
    blocks; resultater, not licenser)
  - "DataSpring giver virksomheden ét sandt sæt tal, hentet fra de systemer, den allerede bruger." ("ét" with
    the accent when it means one)
  - "SiteSpring driver virksomhedens hjemmeside: hurtig, let at finde og holdt opdateret af agenter."
    (hjemmeside, not CMS)
  - "BrainSpring samler det, virksomheden ved, ét sted: fakta, hvordan arbejdet gøres, og hvem der må se hvad."
- **The same rules hold in Danish:** no hype words, no em dashes, short sentences. Write Danish first; don't
  translate English sentence by sentence.

## 6. In product chrome

Stricter than the rest, consistent with sections 7 and 9 of the [brand guideline](README.md):

- **No em dashes.** Use a full stop, comma, colon or parentheses. An en dash in a range is fine.
- **No exclamation marks and no emoji.**
- **Buttons say what happens,** verb first, in the package's sentence case: "Log ind", "Gå til betaling",
  "Prøv igen", "Log ud af Forspring". Never "OK", "Submit" or "Click here".
- **Errors say what happened and what to do next.** The refusals in `@forspring/ui/refusals` have three
  parts: a title, one or two sentences on what it means, and the fix. "Du er ikke logget ind" / "Siden kræver,
  at du er logget ind med din Forspring-konto." / "Log ind, så kommer du tilbage hertil." Never blame the
  person, and never an error with no way forward.
- **Use the package's words.** The bar, the standard pages and the refusals are the package's, in Danish and
  English (ui D-17); a product does not reword them.
- **Status is never colour alone** (section 9): a word with every icon or signal.
- **No marketing in the chrome.** No claims, no hype, no "AI-powered" labels. Say what the screen does.

## 7. When in doubt

- **The letter wins.** If a line would not sit comfortably in the founders' letter, rewrite it.
- **Ask before inventing a claim.** A product capability, a number, a customer or a promise that is not in
  vision.md, offer.md or a decision goes to Peter (through the hub's queue) before it goes in copy.
- **A missing text is added to vision.md,** not written in a page. Ask lead-brand.
